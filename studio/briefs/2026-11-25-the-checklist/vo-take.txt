[S1] NARRATOR: In 1935, the most advanced airplane in the world crashed on its first big test flight.
[S2] NARRATOR: Nothing was broken. The pilot was an experienced army test pilot.
[S3] NARRATOR: The controls were still locked. One forgotten step, among too many to remember.
[S4] NARRATOR: So pilots didn't try to fly better. They wrote the steps down. The checklist was born, and that plane became the B-17.
[S5] NARRATOR: When work gets complex enough, memory stops being a skill. It becomes a risk.
[S6] NARRATOR: Medicine is that complex. Especially between visits.
[S7] NARRATOR: Surgeons proved it once. A one-page checklist across eight hospitals cut deaths after surgery from one and a half percent to under one.
[S8] NARRATOR: Now imagine that for the months between visits. Every patient's next step, held by something more reliable than memory.
[S9] NARRATOR: A written plan for each patient. What to check. When to act. Who to call.
[S10] NARRATOR: The best pilot in 1935 didn't need more skill. He needed a list. So does the best doctor.
