One percent a day sounds like nothing.

Do it every day for a year: 1.01^365 ≈ 37.8. Lose one percent a day: 0.99^365 ≈ 0.03. Nothing happens. Then everything does.

It's the same law behind a patient's year. A skipped pill. A little more salt. A kilo here. A reading creeping up. Nobody sees one percent. By the next visit, it looks like a crisis.

The doctor who sees the trend can turn it while it's small, and small turns compound too. That's a good plan: small daily steps, a signal when the line bends, and someone watching the line with you.
