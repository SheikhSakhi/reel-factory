# Facts used (one trial)

ACCOMPLISH trial (Comparative Effectiveness of Readmission Reduction Interventions for Individuals with Sepsis or Pneumonia). Yende S, et al. JAMA Network Open, published 11 June 2026; 9(6); doi 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.16641. University of Pittsburgh / UPMC.
Source used: UPMC news release, https://www.upmc.com/media/news/061126-remote-patient-monitoring-sepsis-readmissions-trial

1. 1,286 adults (sepsis or pneumonia survivors), enrolled over nearly four years.
2. Remote patient monitoring tracked vital signs and symptoms from home; four strategies: short or long symptom questionnaires x standard or enhanced clinical response teams; compared with usual post-discharge care.
3. 90-day readmission: 36.3% to 44.2% across RPM arms vs 37.8% usual care; "none of the differences reached statistical significance."
4. "Older patients who received RPM had paradoxically higher readmission rates" (no subgroup numbers in the release).
5. Investigators: strong usual care (nurse follow-up, primary care) may explain the lack of added benefit.

S6-S7 ("someone still has to decide what each number means"; "more data isn't more care") are the reel's interpretation, not trial findings. The comparison is with good usual care, not with no follow-up; the reel says "usual follow-up".
