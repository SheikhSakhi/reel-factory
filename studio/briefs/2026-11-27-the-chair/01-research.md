# Research

1. **Readmissions and the missing visit.** Jencks SF, Williams MV, Coleman EA. Rehospitalizations among patients in the Medicare fee-for-service program. N Engl J Med 2009;360:1418-28. Nearly 12 million Medicare beneficiaries discharged in 2003-04: 19.6% rehospitalized within 30 days. For 50.1% of those rehospitalized within 30 days (52.0% for heart failure) there was no bill for an outpatient visit between discharge and readmission.
   Sources: https://commonwealthfund.org/publications/journal-article/2009/apr/rehospitalizations-among-patients-medicare-fee-service ; https://www.commonwealthfund.org/press-release/2009/new-study-20-percent-hospitalized-medicare-patients-readmitted-hospital-within
   Note: the "26.9%" often quoted next to heart failure in summaries is NOT a heart-failure readmission rate; not used.
2. **Weight as the warning sign.** Heart Failure Matters (ESC Heart Failure Association patient site): weigh daily at the same time; tell your doctor or nurse if weight rises by more than 2 kg in 3 days. Other bodies use tighter triggers (e.g. 1 kg overnight), so the reel says "guidance" and the caption says the care team sets the personal line.
   Source: https://www.heartfailurematters.org/warning-signs/rapid-weight-gain/
3. **Breathless lying flat, swollen ankles:** general warning signs of fluid build-up in heart failure (same site). In the story the daughter calls the doctor at once; the caption adds: breathless at rest = emergency number.

Illustrative story; no individual advice, no drug names or doses.
